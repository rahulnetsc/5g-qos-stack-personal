## 1. G1 — "Driving a robot feels immediate while the rest of the fleet works"

### 1.1 The experiment
GT-1.1, *teleop responsiveness under fleet load*: two robots are driven —
each receives a 20 Hz `cmd_vel` stream on 5QI 1 **downlink** (the
instrument) while uplinking its camera at nominal; the last robot pulls a
saturating 5QI-9 DL firmware image so the downlink is genuinely loaded;
every other robot runs its committed profile. The question is whether
downlink command delivery stays immediate as the fleet grows and as the
committed load scales.

### 1.2 Tests run
| part | criterion |
|---|---|
| 1 | `cmd_vel` one-way p98 ≤ 95 ms (RAN PDB); p99.9 and max reported |
| 2 | zero command gaps ≥ 200 ms at the receiver (a dropped command widens the gap around it — this is the half a percentile cannot see) |

A driven robot with fewer than two completions fails both parts.
**Stress axes:** fleet size N ∈ {4 … 24} at committed ×1.0; committed load
×{0.5 … 3.0} at N = 6; each at cap 4 (the deployment's) and cap 2 (this
carrier's own derivation). 10 seeds, 40 000 slots.
`scripts/g1_stress.py --fixed-n 6 --caps 4,2`, 1 280 runs.

### 1.3 Results

**Passes of 10, parts 1 / 2 — every cell, every arm, both caps: 10/10.**
Boundaries: fleet axis 24 on all four arms at both caps; load axis ×3.0 on
all four arms at both caps. 0 breaches in 1 280 runs.

**cmd_vel p98 (median over seeds / worst seed, ms) and worst command gap (ms), cap 4 fleet axis**

| arm | N=4 | N=8 | N=12 | N=16 | N=24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PF | 2.50 / 2.50 · 55 | 2.50 / 3.75 · 55 | 2.50 / 3.75 · 55 | 4.00 / 5.00 · 56 | 5.25 / 6.25 · 58 |
| Reservation | 2.50 / 3.75 · 55 | 1.50 / 3.75 · 55 | 3.75 / 3.75 · 56 | 2.62 / 3.75 · 100 | 2.75 / 3.75 · 56 |
| TwoTier | 3.12 / 3.75 · 55 | 2.50 / 3.75 · 55 | 3.75 / 3.75 · 100 | 1.50 / 3.75 · 55 | 2.50 / 3.75 · 55 |
| ProtoRRageD2 | 2.50 / 3.75 · 55 | 2.50 / 3.75 · 55 | 2.00 / 3.75 · 55 | 3.12 / 3.75 · 55 | 2.50 / 3.75 · 56 |

At cap 2 the picture is the same except PF, which degrades with fleet size
(p98 median 2.50 → **9.00 ms**, worst 10.25 ms at N = 24) while the three
QoS-keyed arms stay at 2.5–3.75 ms. Worst command gap anywhere in the
campaign: 104 ms, against the 200 ms bound. Load axis: flat on every arm
from ×0.5 to ×3.0.

### 1.4 Conclusion
**G1 passes on every arm with 0 breaches of 1 280 runs**, and the margins
are large: the worst p98 anywhere is 10.25 ms against 95, the worst gap
104 ms against 200. The only arm-differentiating signal is PF's mild
degradation with fleet size at cap 2, which the three arms with a DL
deadline tier do not show — TwoTier's DL key ranks `has_gbr` then `pdb_ms`
above the composite, and it keeps `cmd_vel` at 2.5–3.75 ms at any fleet
size. `ProtoRRageD2` is indistinguishable from TwoTier here, as it should
be: its edits are uplink-only and the downlink scheduler is the port's.
Retry alignment did not change the verdict (pre-alignment: also
0 of 960 breaches).

## 2. G2 — "The master disconnects: does every robot stop in time?"

### 2.1 The experiment
GT-1.2, *emergency stop under worst case*: a scripted master-disconnect
sends a 40 B STOP datagram on 5QI 85 **downlink** to `n_stop` robots in the
same slot — simultaneity is structural (`scripted_burst` on a shared slot
list), because same-slot DL contention between the highest-priority packets
in the cell is what the clause exercises. Every robot runs its full
committed profile; the cell is saturated in both directions with 5QI 9. 30
trials per run with randomised trigger phase within the frame.

### 2.2 Tests run
The clause is *100 % of STOPs ≤ 100 ms one-way at every asset in every
trial; demonstrated miss-rate bound per §5.3*. A STOP is scored as **missed
when it does not complete inside its trial window** — the 5QI-85 bearer's
own PDB is 5 ms and `expire()` discards past it, so a late STOP never
enters a latency statistic; a miss is non-delivery, and the honest
statement is a Clopper–Pearson upper limit on the miss rate, never a
percentile. **Stress axes:** simultaneous-STOP count ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8} at
N = 12; fleet size N ∈ {4, 6, 7, 8, 12, 16} × committed load
×{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0} at STOP = 2; each at cap 4 and cap 2. 10 seeds,
40 000 slots. `scripts/g2_stress.py --fixed-n 12 --fixed-stop 2 --caps 4,2`,
2 160 runs, 146 400 STOP events.

### 2.3 Results

**Campaign:** 451 missed of 146 400 STOP events; miss-rate 3.08 × 10⁻³,
**95 % upper bound 3.33 × 10⁻³**. Every delivered STOP arrived within
5.25 ms (the bearer's PDB plus one slot) on every arm — nothing is late,
things are missing.

**Missed / STOP events, whole campaign per arm**

|---|---|---|
| PF | 45 / 18 300 | **219** / 18 300 |
| Reservation | 28 / 18 300 | 49 / 18 300 |
| TwoTier | 20 / 18 300 | 28 / 18 300 |
| ProtoRRageD2 | **16** / 18 300 | 46 / 18 300 |

**Simultaneous-STOP axis at N = 12 (missed / events)**

| arm | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | · | cap 2: 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 |

### 2.4 Conclusion
**G2 fails its clause on every arm** — no arm reaches zero misses, and the
demonstrated bound is 3.3 × 10⁻³. What decides is the number of robots
stopping **at once** (misses rise with `n_stop` on every arm, steeply at
cap 2 where the DCI cap can serve only two of eight STOPs per slot) and,
on PF only, fleet size: at cap 2 PF's misses climb from 1 to **120** per
2 400 across the fleet axis while the three arms with a DL deadline tier
stay at 0–6. That is the flood-robot demotion mechanism
(`docs/flood-robot-demotion-2026-09-09.md`): PF's single-term key demotes
the robot that just received a large download, and its STOP with it;
TwoTier's DL key ranks `has_gbr` and `pdb_ms` above the composite and
protects it. **This is the downlink evidence for the two-tier design**, and
it survives the retry alignment. `ProtoRRageD2` is a UL-only change, so it
is TwoTier here to within seed noise (16 vs 20 misses at cap 4, 46 vs 28 at
cap 2 — the whole-campaign difference is 26 events in 36 600, inside
the noise the cap-2 STOP-8 cell alone shows).

## 3. G3 — "Can the network make a healthy robot look dead?"

### 3.1 The experiment
GT-2.2, *heartbeat vs neighbour's flood*: Asset A carries its full committed
profile and its 300 B / 100 ms telemetry is the instrument; every robot
carries the same telemetry, a 4 Mbps camera and an 8 Mbps best-effort UL
filler; the last robot additionally sources a 50 Mbps 5QI-9 UL flood and
keeps its own telemetry (so it is an asset, not a pure aggressor). The
question is whether uplink contention can silence a robot's liveness signal.

### 3.2 Tests run
Clause L97, scored as four parts on Asset A's and the flood robot's
telemetry:

| part | criterion | note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | max inter-arrival gap ≤ 500 ms (T_live/4) | the clause as written |
| 1s | longest observable silence ≤ 500 ms, head and tail included | the same bound on a statistic that can see a robot that goes dark and stays dark |
| 2 | zero gaps ≥ 2 s (T_live) in the run; and campaign-wide | |
| 3 | telemetry p98 one-way ≤ 95 ms (RAN PDB) | |

A flow delivering fewer than two messages fails every part. **Stress axis:**
fleet size N ∈ {4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 24} robots, 10 seeds each,
40 000 slots (10 s) per run, plus one 200 000-slot (50 s) cell at N = 6 per
arm. `scripts/g3_stress.py --parts A`.

## 4. G4 — "After a robot goes quiet, its next message still arrives promptly"

### 4.1 The experiment
This is the post-silence study `scripts/g4_postsilence.py` (WP9 §21),
**not** the test plan's GT-2.3 bucket campaign, and the difference is
stated up front. GT-2.3 pauses Asset A's telemetry for {1, 5, 60} s and
asks for first-packet p99 ≤ 300 ms per bucket — 100 cycles per bucket,
~96 min, and still deferred (`docs/deferred-tasks.md` §1; the scripted
pauses were exercised in the pre-alignment G3 campaign's GT-2.3 part,
where every resume completed within 134 ms on every arm). What runs here
scores **every message by the silence that preceded it**, on the
parametric `factory` workload at N = 8 with the duty cycle of every
periodic source stepped {1.0, 0.5, 0.1}: latency is reported per
log-spaced preceding-gap bucket, so no silence threshold is chosen. The
duty axis carries a size confound by construction (`_burstify` grows the
burst by 1/duty at constant mean rate), so the arm comparison is made
**within a duty level**, where every arm sees identical message sizes.

### 4.2 Tests run
| statistic | bound |
|---|---|
| per-message latency p98 by preceding-gap bucket {[0,1), [10,100), [100,1000), [1000,∞)} ms | the clause's 300 ms, read against the ≥ 100 ms buckets (a message after a silence) |
| across arms at one duty: paired difference to PF with bootstrap CI | CI excluding zero = a real difference |

**Stress axis:** duty cycle ∈ {1.0, 0.5, 0.1}; N = 8; 10 seeds; 20 000
slots. 4 arms × 3 duty × 10 seeds = 120 runs, 5 168 flow-bucket rows.

### 4.3 Results

**Post-silence latency p98 (ms) / mean message size, duty 1.0**

| arm | gap [0,1) ms | [10,100) ms | **[100,1000) ms** |
|---|---|---|---|
| PF | 161.5 / 839 B | 10.2 / 633 B | 23.7 / 300 B |
| Reservation | 163.1 / 840 B | 14.4 / 573 B | 26.4 / 300 B |
| TwoTier | 178.1 / 839 B | 28.1 / 633 B | **63.0** / 300 B |
| ProtoRRageD2 | 160.7 / 839 B | 8.5 / 633 B | **19.9** / 300 B |

(The [0,1) bucket is the video flow's in-burst fragments; the [100,1000)
bucket is the 300 B telemetry message after its own 100 ms period — the
clause's case.) At duty 0.1 a [1000, ∞) bucket appears (3 000 B bursts
after ≥ 1 s of silence): PF 78.7, Reservation 57.0, TwoTier 82.2,
ProtoRRageD2 80.4 ms — every arm inside 300 ms.

**Across arms, all messages, paired difference to PF (ms; \* = 95 % CI excludes zero)**

| duty | PF p98 | ProtoRRageD2 − PF | Reservation − PF | TwoTier − PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 62.9 | **−1.8** \* | −0.3 | **+21.0** \* |
| 0.5 | 65.2 | −0.9 \* | −3.2 \* | **+10.0** \* |
| 0.1 | 107.2 | −0.1 | +10.1 \* | +1.4 \* |

### 4.4 Conclusion
**Against the clause's 300 ms, every arm passes on every gap bucket at
every duty level** — the largest post-silence p98 anywhere is 82 ms, at
duty 0.1 on a 3 000 B burst. The arm-differentiating result is TwoTier at
full duty: its message after a 100 ms silence takes **63 ms at p98
against 20–26 ms** on the other three, and across all messages it sits
+21 ms above PF with a CI that excludes zero. That is the same uplink
cadence tail seen in G3 at N = 8 — here it is visible even at the plan's
easiest pause length, and it shrinks as duty falls because a sparser
source is a smaller share of the ranking's backlog. `ProtoRRageD2` is at
or below PF at every duty. **The GT-2.3 buckets (1/5/60 s, p99) remain
unmeasured**; nothing here should be quoted as that clause.

## 11. Overall conclusion

**Ten of the twelve guarantees were measured on the corrected model, on all
four schedulers, in one day's campaigns.** The table is the answer to the
evaluation's two questions — does each guarantee hold, and does the two-tier
design earn its place:

| G | question | PF | Reservation | TwoTier (faithful) | ProtoRRageD2 (divergence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | teleop feels immediate | pass, 0 of 1 280 | pass | pass | pass |
| G2 | every robot stops in time — misses of 36 600 | 264 (**fleet-sensitive**) | 77 | 48 | 62 |
| G3 | liveness — all-parts boundary / campaign silences | 24 / 0 | 6 / 10 | 7 / 25 | **14 / 0** |
| G4 | prompt after silence — p98 after a 100 ms gap | 24 ms | 26 | **63** | **20** |
| G5 | video — admissible fleet / load knee | 14 / none | 10 / none | 4 / ×1.2 | **14 / none** |
| G6 | background can't impair — part A/B of 270, UL flood | 259/251 | 225/241 | **176/176** | 255/258 |
| G7 | containment — clause 1 · B÷MFBR · clause 3 | pass · 1.04× · pass | pass · 2.00× · pass | **fail** · 2.05× · pass | pass · 1.08× · pass |
| G9 | join / recover — catastrophic seeds, faithful column | 6 (at (8, ×2) only) | 8 (same) | **44** | 8 (same) |
| G10 | admissible fleet | 12 | 6 | 7 | **10** |
| G12 | safety telemetry survives overload / order scoreable | pass / no | pass / no | pass / no (violation fraction 0.30 at ×2.0) | pass / no |

> **WITHDRAWN 2026-09-16.** The claim in this paragraph about G1's command
> p98 failing G6's shift test no longer holds. `scripts/g6_isolation.py`'s
> part B compared a pure ratio with no absolute floor; with a floor of 5 %
> of each statistic's own bound, `cmd_vel` p98 passes part B on 30 of 30
> cells on EVERY arm. The failures it describes were moves of 1.5-4.0 ms
> against a 95 ms bound. See `docs/test-definition-changes-2026-09-16.md`
> and `docs/results-cell-2026-09-15.md` section 6.3. The paragraph is kept
> as written, for the record.

1. **On the uplink, both faithful QoS schedulers fail the liveness, video,
   containment and post-silence guarantees that the contract-blind PF
   passes** (G3, G4, G5, G7, and G6's UL half), and TwoTier alone fails
   G9's recoveries. The mechanism is the same everywhere it was traced:
   service cadence, not capacity. Protected uplink plateaus at ~24 Mbps on
   the QoS arms while the same band carries 65 on the other two; the
   coef-ranked order leaves a robot unvisited for seconds, a joiner at the
   bottom, and a heartbeat behind a neighbour's over-driven encoder.
2. **On the downlink, the two-tier design is the one that works.** G1
   passes everywhere, but on G2 PF's single-term key demotes the robot
   that just received a large download and its STOP with it — 264 misses
   against 48–77 — while the three arms with a DL deadline tier are flat in
   fleet size. This survived the retry alignment unchanged and is the
   strongest evidence for the design.
3. **One uplink change closes the gap.** `ProtoRRageD2` — Tier 2 replaced
   on every slot by slots-since-last-grant, the reserve bounded to two
   followers the cap can serve — is PF-equivalent or better on G3, G4, G5,
   G6, G7 and G9, two robots behind PF on G10, and TwoTier on the downlink
   guarantees, with the composite, Tier-1, floors, sizing and the whole
   downlink scheduler untouched. It is a labelled divergence: it says what
   a change would do, never what the product does.
4. **Two guarantees fail on every arm for reasons no scheduler reaches.**
   G2's misses are the 5QI-85 bearer's own 5 ms PDB discarding STOPs the
   network would have delivered (a specification finding); G6's shift test
   fails on G1's command p98 on every arm because a 20 % relative bound on a
   3 ms statistic is a few slots of jitter. Both are statements about the
   clauses.
5. **The retry alignment changed no verdict.** It moved TwoTier's G3 and
   G10 boundaries by one to two robots in its favour and left every
   pass/fail where the pre-alignment campaigns had it — the expected
   signature of a fidelity fix that only touches runs with failed
   transport blocks.

**What this says about the design:** on the uplink the two-tier composite
coefficient — backlog × channel term, urgency folded in — is not what wins
these guarantees; regular visiting is. On the downlink its deadline tier
is exactly what wins. The configured-grant and configuration-scheduler work
that follows should start from that split.

  found that M09 pools roles (Jain across a 24 kbps heartbeat and a 4 Mbps

  is defined as "above the GT-3.2 ceiling", which G5 measured as not
  existing below ×1.5 on two arms. It needs a scenario, a per-role
  statistic and a defined load level before it can run.
- **G11 (soak / ordered degradation)** is not scoreable on three of five

  counters with no C counterpart); its C1/C3 pass over 7.2 M slots is
  pre-alignment and was not re-run.
- **G4 as GT-2.3 specifies it** — first-packet p99 over 1 / 5 / 60 s

  preceding-gap study, and says so.
- **Held-out seeds** were not re-run under the aligned model. Pre-alignment,
  `ProtoRRageD2`'s G3 boundaries reproduced exactly on a disjoint seed set
  while the previous candidate's moved by six; every boundary here is the
  repo's last-N-at-10/10 rule and should be read beside the pass counts.
- **No figure here is registered in `config/published_claims.yml`.** Every
