# G6 — Step 0, established before anything is built

**2026-09-11.** G6: *"Background traffic (logs, firmware) can never impair the
fleet."* Written before the scenario, the runner or any number exists;
predictions in §6 are registered here and scored in G6's own result document.

---

## 1. G6 IS THE FIRST GUARANTEE WITH NO STATISTIC OF ITS OWN

> **G6** — *"With saturating 5QI-9 load added (either direction), every G1/G3/G5
> statistic stays within its bound and shifts by ≤ ▷ +20 % relative."*

It re-scores three other guarantees under an added flood and asks **two
questions of each**, which are independent and can disagree:

| part | question | failure mode it catches |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | does the statistic stay inside its OWN bound? | the flood breaks the guarantee outright |
| **B** | does it shift by more than **+20 % relative**? | the flood degrades it materially while it still passes |

**Part B is the one that needs G6 to exist.** Part A is answerable by running
G1, G3 and G5 with a flood and reading their existing verdicts. Part B is a
*paired ratio*, and a ratio needs a control.

## 2. THE FINDING THAT DECIDES THE DESIGN: there is no no-flood baseline anywhere

**Every one of the three guarantees ALREADY carries 5QI-9 background traffic.**
Measured on their own builders at N = 8:

| guarantee | 5QI-9 flows present | what they are |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** GT-1.1 | **6** (5 UL + 1 DL) | `poisson`, 8 Mbps per UL robot, **50 Mbps DL** on the last |
| **G3** GT-2.2 | **8** (UL) | per-UE filler plus the 50 Mbps saturating flood |
| **G5** GT-3.1 | **1** (UL) | the 50 Mbps saturator on Asset B |

**Two consequences, and both are Step-0 decisions rather than details.**

**(a) The published artefacts are the TREATMENT, not the control.** "Load
*added*" has nothing to be added to, so G6 cannot be scored by re-reading
existing artefacts against each other. It must run **both** conditions itself,
paired on identical seeds, or part B is measuring two different experiments —
the category error this project has recorded three times.

**(b) The existing "background" is not the same thing in all three.** G1's
uplink filler is an 8 Mbps Poisson source per robot; G3's and G5's are 50 Mbps
saturators. A Poisson filler at 8 Mbps is *offered load*, not *saturation*, so
G1's published numbers are not "with a saturating flood" in the sense the
clause means. **Calling all three "with background" and differencing them would
compare a saturator against a trickle.**

## 3. "SHIFTS BY ≤ +20 %" HAS NO DIRECTION, AND AN UNDIRECTED READING IS WRONG

A statistic can **improve** under added load, and G3's own campaign shows it:
adding contention changes which robot wins the sort, and worst-case silence has
moved *down* between conditions in this project's measurements.

* read as `|Δ| ≤ 20 %`, a run that got 30 % BETTER fails;
* read as signed `Δ ≤ +20 %`, an improvement passes, which is right.

**So the bound is ONE-SIDED and in the direction of harm**, and "harm" is
per-metric because the panel's own `direction` field already records whether
higher or lower is better (`lower_better` for latency and gaps,
`higher_better` for completeness and goodput). **The sign is read from the
panel, never hardcoded per metric here** — restating it is how a threshold
silently inverts for one metric.

**And a relative shift is undefined on a statistic whose baseline is zero.**
G3's part 2 counts campaign silences ≥ 2 s and the faithful arm's baseline can
be 0; G5's part 4 counts harmed telemetry flows. `(x - 0) / 0` is not a 20 %
question. **For a count with a zero baseline the criterion is ABSOLUTE — the
count must not rise — and that is stated per metric in the runner rather than
divided and hoped for.**

## 4. THE POPULATION, INHERITED RATHER THAN RE-DECIDED

G6 scores **exactly the instruments G1, G3 and G5 already fixed**, and must not
re-choose them:

* **G1** — the driven robot's DOWNLINK `cmd_vel` on 5QI 1 (p98 and gap count);
* **G3** — Asset A's UPLINK telemetry on 5QI 1 (max gap, campaign silences,
  observable silence);
* **G5** — Asset A's UPLINK camera on 5QI 2 (set completeness, frame age,
  windowed goodput floor) **plus** part 4's telemetry collateral.

**The flood itself is never scored**, in either direction. `Population.
protected_fleet()` already excludes 5QI 8/9, so this is inherited machinery, not
a new rule — but the runner still reports the flood's own throughput, because
*"bg gets residual only"* is part of GT-4.1's pass line and a flood that got
nothing would mean the test never ran.

## 5. WHICH SCENARIOS EXIST, AND WHAT IS BUILT

**None of GT-4's cells exist.** What exists is better than a blank page:
three instrument scenarios that are correct and already validated.

**So G6 is built as a TRANSFORMATION, not a fourth scenario family.**
`sim/scenarios/g6.py` takes a base scenario from G1, G3 or G5 and returns it
with the saturating 5QI-9 flow **removed** (the control) or **added in a named
direction** (the treatment). Three reasons this is the right shape:

1. **The instrument stays byte-identical between conditions.** A paired delta is
   only a delta if nothing else moved, and rebuilding the scenario from
   parameters cannot guarantee that.
2. **It inherits every instrument decision** those three modules already
   defend — the canonical LCG ordering, offered ≥ GFBR, the fixed instrument
   robot.
3. **GT-4.2 is a DIRECTION, not a new workload.** *"A firmware push to one robot
   cannot blunt another robot's controls"* is the same cell with the saturator
   on the downlink.

**GT-4.2 also asks for something no guarantee has measured: the firmware's own
completion time for a fixed 500 MB image**, because clients ask how long updates
take with the fleet live. That is a **finite transfer**, not a saturator, so it
needs a distinct source kind and a completion instrument, and it is the one
genuinely new mechanism in G6.

## 6. THE GRID, ITS COST, AND THE AXES

**The axes are inherited, and the CONDITION is the new one.**

| axis | levels | why |
|---|---|---|
| **condition** | `{none, UL flood, DL flood}` | the clause's own "either direction", plus the control part B requires |
| **instrument** | `{G1 cmd, G3 telemetry, G5 video}` | the clause names exactly these three |
| **fleet size** | `{4, 7, 12}` | brackets G10's re-measured boundaries (PF 12 / Res 6 / TwoTier 7) without paying for the full nine-point axis |

**Cap 4 only**, the deployment's value. **Arms: the three faithful plus the
Proto arm**, per the standing rule that every campaign scores it.

**Cost, measured rather than projected.** Recent runs on this machine: G1 320
runs in 241 s and G2 540 runs in 414 s at 14 workers — **~52 000 slots/s**, and
the box has **32 cores**, so ~28 workers is the right pool and roughly doubles
that.

| | runs | slots | wall @14 workers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 conditions × 3 instruments × 3 N × 4 arms × 10 seeds | **1 080** | 43.2 M | **~14 min** |

**Affordable, and the control is what buys it.** Two thirds of those runs are
the flood conditions and one third is the control; skipping the control would
save 5 minutes and make part B unanswerable.

## 7. REGISTERED PREDICTIONS, written before any G6 number exists

| | prediction | falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| **P-G6-a** | **Part A passes far more often than part B.** Staying inside a bound is a weaker test than shifting by less than a fifth, and the guarantees have large headroom on some statistics (G1's worst p98 is 3.00 ms against a 95 ms bound) | part B passes wherever part A does |
| **P-G6-b** | **The DL flood harms G1 and barely touches G3/G5.** G1's instrument is downlink; G3's and G5's are uplink, and reach a DL flood only through the HARQ draw-order coupling | the DL flood moves an uplink instrument as much as the downlink one |
| **P-G6-c** | **PF fails part B on G1 where the QoS arms pass.** PF's key is one term with a per-UE rate average shared across directions, so a robot that just received a firmware burst is demoted and its own command inherits the penalty — measured in G2, where the download-carrying robot held 55.9 % of PF's missed STOPs | PF's G1 shift is no worse than the QoS arms' |
| **P-G6-d** | **The +20 % criterion is the binding one for the Proto arm, not the bound.** It improves G3 and G5 substantially, and a large improvement is a large *shift*; a signed reading must let it pass, so this prediction is really a check that §3's sign convention was implemented | the Proto arm fails part B on a metric where it improved |
| **P-G6-e** | **G5's frame age is the first statistic to fail part A under the UL flood**, on every arm — it is already the tightest of the three (p95 114.5 ms against a 66.67 ms bound on TwoTier at N = 7 with no extra flood) | some other statistic fails part A at a smaller fleet |

**P-G6-d is the one worth most as a process check.** If it fails, §3's
one-sided reading was not implemented and every part-B number is suspect.

## 8. WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT IN SCOPE

* **GT-4.3** validates **G7**, not G6, and is already run as a regression check.
* **G4** stays deferred (`docs/deferred-tasks.md` §1).
* **The 22 stale claim stamps** stay deferred (§2 there); G6 publishes no claim
  until they are resolved.
* **The 500 MB firmware completion time** is specified here and built with
  GT-4.2, but it is a *characterisation deliverable* with no pass line, so it
  cannot fail the guarantee — stated so it is not later read as one.
