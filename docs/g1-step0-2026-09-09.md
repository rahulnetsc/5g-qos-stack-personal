## (a) The clause has TWO pass criteria — CONFIRMED, and the text is exact

`docs/IA_P5G_Factory_Guarantee_Test_Plan.md`:

- **L95** (the catalogue row): *"cmd_vel one-way p98 ≤ RAN PDB (▷ 95 ms of
  the 100 ms 5QI-1 budget, §5); p99.9 reported."*
- **GT-1.1** (the test): *"KPIs/pass: cmd_vel one-way p98 ≤ RAN PDB; p99.9
  and max reported; **zero command gaps ≥ 200 ms**."*

**Two bounds, two reported figures, and nothing else.** I read GT-1's whole
family (GT-1.1/1.2/1.3) and §4's design principles; no further G1 criterion
exists. GT-1.2 (100 % of STOPs ≤ 100 ms) and GT-1.3 (first-command p99 ≤
150 ms per silence bucket) belong to G2 and to G1's *sporadic* sub-test and
are not in scope here.

**The gap criterion is independent, and the reason is the one given:** p98
ranges over DELIVERED messages. `sim/scorecard.py::_m01_latency_percentiles`
computes it over `message_count` fully-delivered messages and *excludes* a
flow that delivered none — so a scheduler that drops commands outright
improves its own percentile. The gap statistic is receiver-side
inter-arrival, so a dropped command widens the gap that surrounds it. **The
two criteria fail in opposite directions and neither implies the other.**

**One hole this campaign closes explicitly:** `_m03_liveness_gap_
distribution` excludes a flow with fewer than two completions from the
worst-gap contest. For G1 that is the wrong disposition — **a cmd_vel flow
with fewer than two completions is a FAILURE, not an exclusion** — so G1's
own scorer treats it as one rather than inheriting M03's rule.

## (c) The scenario did not exist — CONFIRMED; and the comparator claim is confirmed in its OBSERVATION, REFUTED in its MECHANISM

### (c1) No GT-1.1 cell existed

GT-1.1 needs Asset A driven on DL cmd_vel while uplinking camera, and Asset
B on full committed UL **plus a saturating 5QI-9 DL firmware pull**. Nothing
in the repo has a saturating downlink flow at all. The parametric mix's only
DL flow is a 120 B / 50 ms control loop; measured DL delivered throughput is
a fraction of the carrier and DL p98 is ~3 ms. That is precisely the
condition GT-1.1's own text calls *"an idle DL link measures nothing"*.

Built as **`sim/scenarios/g1.py::build_gt11_scenario`** — see §1 below for
the consumer/duplication/liveness statement.

### (c2) TwoTier's DL comparator — the observation reproduces

`docs/dl-comparator-result-2026-09-07.md` reported TwoTier's DL key as
`(1, 100, -0.0)` for every candidate, its ordering therefore declaration
order. Re-measured on current code (N=6, 6,000 slots, cap 4, RA+SRB, DL
rank trace):

| variant | Reservation DL | TwoTier DL | TwoTier distinct DL `coef` values |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** as-built (idle DL) | `-coef` 42.3 % · `pdb_ms` 16.9 % · TIED 40.7 % | `pdb_ms` 15.6 % · **TIED 84.4 %** | **1** (`0.0`) |
| **B** + saturating 5QI-9 DL pull | `-coef` 46.3 % · TIED 37.4 % | `pdb_ms` 36.3 % · `-coef` 2.1 % · TIED 61.6 % | 231 |
| **C** + 5QI-1 DL **GBR** cmd_vel | **`has_gbr` 16.3 %** · `-coef` 40.2 % · TIED 26.1 % | **`has_gbr` 14.4 %** · `pdb_ms` 29.3 % · `-coef` 5.8 % · TIED 50.5 % | 219 |

**Confirmed: as built, TwoTier's downlink coefficient takes exactly one
value and its ordering is declaration order on 84 % of adjacencies.**

### (c3) …but the recorded REASON is wrong, and the correction matters

The 2026-09-07 note attributed the zero to *"`sum_q` is Tier-1's virtual
queue — zero for a flow with no GBR target"*. **That is not the mechanism.**
Per-UE, variant B (only UE 6 carries the flood):

| UE | DL 5QIs | distinct `coef` | non-zero | `has_gbr` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | `[82]` | **1** | **0** | 0.0 |
| 6 | `[9, 82]` | 231 | 230 | 0.0 |

**UE 6's flood has no GBR target and its coefficient reaches 4.8e5.** So a
GBR target is neither necessary nor sufficient for a non-zero `coef`.

The real mechanism is the VQ's own ceiling
(`two_tier.py::_update_vq_dl`, `ia_p5g_scheduler.c:1835-1894`):

```
ceiling = max(0, min(arr_W, target_W) - del_W)
```

**`vq_dl` is a SERVICE DEFICIT.** A flow that is never behind has `del_W ≈
arr_W`, so its ceiling collapses to zero and its coefficient with it. The
120 B / 50 ms control loop is always fully delivered, on every UE, at every
load — hence exactly one value. Tier-1 gives *every* flow with demand a
target, GBR or not (`tier1.py`: `gbr_idx` gates only the GBR *slack* column,
not the rate column).

### (c4) And the consequence for G1 is the one predicted, by a different route

Variant C, per UE: the two driven UEs show `has_gbr = 1.0` **always** and
`coef = 0.0` **always**. So:

- **A DL background flow alone does not make the comparator discriminate for
  a command flow.** It un-constants the *flooded* UE's coefficient only; the
  command-carrying UEs stay at one value. The registered expectation holds.
- **What discriminates for a command flow is the GBR target**, through
  `has_gbr` — a tier above `coef`, and dead on every scenario this repo had.
- **`-coef` is not the route and would not have been**, which is why the
  recorded mechanism mattered: it named the wrong tier.

**`docs/dl-comparator-result-2026-09-07.md`'s "Why" section is superseded by
this section.** Its measurements stand; its explanation does not.

## 1. What was added, and what it makes live

Per the standing rule (`write-for-the-system-not-the-site`), for each
addition: who consumes it, what becomes live that was inert, what it
duplicates.

### `sim/scenarios/g1.py` (new)

- **Consumers:** `scripts/g1_stress.py`; `sim/tests/test_flow_key_collision_sweep.py`
  (both the collision sweep and its coverage assertion);
  `sim/tests/test_workload.py`'s GBR-offered invariant, which reaches it
  through the same `_cases()`.
- **Becomes live that was inert:** TwoTier's and Reservation's **downlink
  `has_gbr` ranking tier**, which decided 0 % of adjacencies on every
  scenario in the repo and now decides **11.9 % (TwoTier) / 13.1 %
  (Reservation) of downlink adjacencies at the realistic fleet size** in the
  full campaign. Also the DL half of the
  M-6 per-slot UE cap, which had no contended downlink to bind on.
- **Duplicates:** nothing. It reuses `sim/workload.py::scale_committed_load`
  and `min_bytes_per_period_for_gfbr` rather than restating either, and
  takes its camera/telemetry shapes from `sim/parametric.py`'s factory mix
  so the two workloads' uplink halves are the same shape. It is the fourth
  guarantee-specific builder beside `g9.py`, `g11.py`, `g12.py`, not a new
  pattern.
- **Not added to `sim/parametric.py`:** that module promises "one workload
  *shape* with continuously varying knobs", and GT-1.1 needs a different
  shape (a saturating downlink, a fixed instrument population, and 5QI 1 on
  DL). Three flags would have hidden a second workload inside the first.

### `delay_p999_ms` / `delay_max_ms` (`sim/messages.py`, `sim/driver.py`, `sim/run_record.py`)

- **Consumers:** every `RunRecord`, so any guarantee that reports a tail can
  read them; `scripts/g1_stress.py` is the first.
- **Why in the record and not in G1's runner:** GT-1.1 asks for them as
  *reported* figures. Computing them privately in one runner would leave the
  next guarantee that needs a max to build the same hook again.
- **Corpus:** a **shape-only** re-baseline, and this is checkable rather than
  asserted. `--check` before capture: **912 mismatch lines, 912 of them
  `MISSING in baseline`, over exactly two new keys, and ZERO numbers moved.**
  456 flow-records × 2 keys = 912.

## 3. Two decisions taken deliberately, recorded rather than left implicit

**`sim/scorecard.py` was NOT touched.** M01's value dict still carries
`{p50, p95, p98, p99, flow}` and does not surface the two new tail figures.
The panel is pre-registered, and widening an already-shipped metric's output
inside an unrelated campaign's diff is the attribution failure the
one-fidelity-change-per-commit rule exists to prevent. G1's runner reads
`FlowRecord.delay_p999_ms` / `delay_max_ms` directly, which is what it needs.
**If M01 should report them, that is its own commit** — the same disposition
`docs/wp7-plan.md` records for M04.

**The category guard for scripted schedules caught the new builder, and it
was exempted rather than silenced.** `test_EVERY_scenario_builder_that_takes_a_horizon_guards_its_schedule`
failed on `g1.py::build_gt11_scenario` the moment it existed. GT-1.1's cell
is steady state — every flow runs slot 0 to end, nothing is scripted to fire
at a slot — so there is no schedule a short horizon could truncate, and it
joins `g12` in `NO_SCHEDULE` **with its reason written down**. The guard's own
message states the standard: *"'no schedule' is fine, 'no schedule silently'
is the finding."*

**The horizon still matters for G1, for a different reason**, and that reason
lives where it belongs: at 20 Hz the horizon sets how many commands a
percentile ranges over, and `scripts/g1_stress.py::HORIZON_SLOTS` records why
40,000 rather than G9/G12's 20,000. A sample-size constraint is not a
truncation constraint, and the schedule guard is the wrong instrument for it.
