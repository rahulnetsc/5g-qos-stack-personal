## 0. Configuration common to every run

- **Code:** HEAD `1f5d105` — the commit that moved every guarantee builder
  onto `sim/scenarios/deployed_cell.py`. Schedulers, driver, link model and
  traffic sources are unchanged from the 2026-09-14 campaigns.
- **Cell** (`docs/deployed-cell-2026-09-15.md`, from `docs/conf/gnbx310.conf`):
  numerology 1 (30 kHz, 0.5 ms slots), 106 PRB (40 MHz, n78), TDD `DDSUU`
  every 2.5 ms with the special slot split 6 DL / 2 gap / 6 UL symbols —
  34 of 70 symbols per period in each direction (48.6 %). Single-layer link
  model (the conf's two MIMO layers are not modelled — a recorded
  divergence); per-slot UE cap 4; `cqi_delay_slots = 8`; random access and
  SRBs on; 10 seeds per point from `regime_sweep.paired_seeds`, seed-base 0.
- **What the cell change does to capacity, from structure** (the link
  model's bits per PRB at the scenarios' 20 dB, times the PRB-symbols each
  direction gets per second, before HARQ, overhead and the cap):

  | cell | uplink ceiling | downlink ceiling |
  |---|---|---|
  | previous: 55 PRB, μ = 2, `DSUUU` 3/2/9 | 94.2 Mbps | 31.4 Mbps |
  | **deployed: 106 PRB, μ = 1, `DDSUU` 6/2/6** | **60.5 Mbps** | **60.5 Mbps** |

  So every uplink guarantee is measured on **64 % of the uplink it had**,
  and every downlink guarantee on **1.9× the downlink**. Read the deltas
  against the previous campaign with that in mind: an uplink boundary
  moving down is the cell before it is the scheduler.
- **Arms:** `PF`, `Reservation` (faithful port), `TwoTier` (faithful port),
  `ProtoRRageD2` (labelled divergence: the two-tier port with its UL Tier-2
  replaced by slots-since-last-UL-grant and the FIX-2 reserve bounded to two
  followers). `+CG` = restricted Type 2 configured grant on the heartbeat,
  `+CGt` = the same with period and phase taken from the declared traffic.
- **Horizons:** the same seconds as before (10 s / 5 s), which is now
  20 000 / 10 000 slots.
- **Platform:** Windows 11, 23 worker processes, `uv run`.

## 3. G3 — "Can the network make a healthy robot look dead?"

### 3.1 The experiment
GT-2.2, *heartbeat vs neighbour's flood*: Asset A carries its full committed
profile and its 300 B / 100 ms telemetry is the instrument; every robot
carries the same telemetry, a 4 Mbps camera and an 8 Mbps best-effort UL
filler; the last robot additionally sources a 50 Mbps 5QI-9 UL flood and
keeps its own telemetry. The question is whether uplink contention can
silence a robot's liveness signal. Unchanged from the previous campaign
except for the cell.

### 3.2 Tests run
Clause L97 as four parts on Asset A's and the flood robot's telemetry —
part 1 max inter-arrival gap ≤ 500 ms; part 1s the same bound on the longest
observable silence, head and tail included; part 2 zero gaps ≥ 2 s, per run
and campaign-wide; part 3 telemetry p98 ≤ 95 ms. Fleet size
N ∈ {4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 24}, 10 seeds each, 20 000 slots (10 s).
`scripts/g3_stress.py --parts A --fixed-n 6 --caps 4 --seeds 10`.

PF's protected uplink stops growing at **43.4–43.8 Mbps from N = 12** — the
cell's uplink is full of protected traffic there (offered protected load at
N = 12 is 12 × 4.024 = 48.3 Mbps against a 60.5 Mbps raw ceiling, before the
10 % HARQ target, the flood robot's share and the SR path). On the previous
cell the same arm tracked the offered load to 66 Mbps at N = 24.

**Campaign-wide part 2 (zero gaps ≥ 2 s over every run):** PF **PASS**
(0 of 37 910 gaps), Reservation FAIL (3 of 30 219), TwoTier FAIL (30 of
35 075), ProtoRRageD2 **PASS** (0 of 38 106).

### 3.4 Conclusion
**The verdict on liveness does not move with the cell: both faithful QoS
arms fail, PF and the Proto arm pass, for the same reason as before.**
TwoTier's protected uplink plateaus at 19–33 Mbps from N = 8 while PF
carries 40–44 on the same seeds, and its worst silences are 8–10 s at every
N ≥ 10; that is service cadence, not capacity, and `ProtoRRageD2` removes
it (campaign silences 30 → 0, worst silence ≤ 494 ms at N = 24).

**What the cell changes is part 3, and it changes it for every arm.** On
the previous cell PF met the 95 ms p98 to N = 24 and the Proto arm to
N = 14; here both drop to N = 10, with p98 pinned at 97–99 ms (the
expiry ceiling) from N = 12 on PF and from N = 16 on the Proto arm. That is
capacity — the protected offered load crosses what 64 % of the old uplink
delivers — and it shows in PF's messages missing (0 on the previous cell,
34–96 per fleet size here from N = 12). Reservation's part-3 numbers look
better at N = 12–16 (p98 22–35 ms) only because it delivers 800–900 fewer
messages: a percentile over delivered messages improves when messages are
dropped, the same shape as G1's finding.

So on this cell the liveness guarantee separates the schedulers (cadence)
and the latency guarantee separates fleet sizes (capacity). The
all-parts boundary is **10 robots on PF and the Proto arm, 6 on TwoTier,
4 on Reservation**.
