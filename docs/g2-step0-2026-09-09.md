# G2 Step 0 — establish before building

**2026-09-09.** G2 is the safety-critical path and currently reads **PASS
10/10 at ~19–25× margin**. This asks whether that row could have failed.

**It could not.** The statistic is structurally incapable of exceeding the
bearer's own 5 ms discard, against a 100 ms bound — and the arm that loses
STOPs and the arm that loses none score the same.

---

## (a) The clause, enumerated — and one part nobody scored

**Test plan L96:** *"100 % of STOP events ≤ ▷ 100 ms across all trials;
demonstrated miss-rate bound stated per §5.3."*
**GT-1.2 KPIs/pass:** *"100 % of STOPs ≤ 100 ms one-way at both assets in
every trial; per-trial worst asset recorded; final Guarantee-Sheet row states
the demonstrated miss-rate bound."*
**Catalogue statement:** *"A STOP always lands, **on every ground robot**,
fast — **even at worst-case load**."*

Six parts, and the last two are the ones no G2 run has scored:

| # | part | scored today? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | latency ≤ **100 ms** | as **p98**, not a maximum — see below |
| 2 | **100 %** of events, i.e. a MAXIMUM over trials | **no** — a percentile was substituted |
| 3 | **"at both assets"** — the per-trial statistic is the WORST asset | **no** — no trial structure exists |
| 4 | **per-trial worst asset recorded** (a distribution) | **no** |
| 5 | **demonstrated miss-rate bound per §5.3** | **no** — no rate is computed at all |
| 6 | **"on every ground robot"**, **"even at worst-case load"** | **no** — see (b) |

### The statistic is a MAXIMUM, and the substitution is not cosmetic

`docs/scorecard-audit-2026-09-07.md` row 7 already flagged
*"**p98 substituted for a maximum**"* and *"pass-only"*. **Confirmed and
discarded.** A p98 over trials tolerates 2 % of STOPs missing; the clause
tolerates none. On 30 trials × 2 assets, p98 is the 2nd-largest of 60 — it
would report a pass with one robot un-stopped.

### §5.3 — how the bound is computed, read rather than recalled

> *"To demonstrate a miss-rate ≤ ε at 95 % confidence with zero observed
> misses, you need **n ≥ 3/ε** samples … **event guarantees (G2, G4)
> accumulate trials across the whole campaign** and the Guarantee Sheet
> states the honestly demonstrable bound (e.g. *"0 misses in 1 240 STOP
> trials → miss-rate ≤ 2.4 × 10⁻³ at 95 % confidence"*)."*

**So the answer G2 owes is a RATE WITH A CONFIDENCE, not a pass/fail**, and
more trials is a strictly better answer:

- **zero misses in `n` trials → miss-rate ≤ 3/n at 95 % confidence** (the
  rule of three).
- **`k > 0` misses is NOT covered by the rule of three.** It is the k=0
  special case of a binomial upper limit; with misses observed the honest
  statement is a Clopper–Pearson upper bound, and this campaign states which
  it used rather than stretching 3/n past its own condition.
- **A trial is one master-disconnect**, so `n` = trials × robots stopped, and
  the campaign accumulates across seeds, arms reported separately.

## (b) The scenario did not exist — and the missing part is the mechanism

GT-1.2 specifies: both assets at full committed profile; the cell **saturated
both directions** with 5QI-9; a **scripted master-disconnect** triggering
**simultaneous** STOP datagrams; **30 trials/run, ≥ 10 runs, randomised
trigger phase within the frame**.

**What exists is one Poisson STOP flow at nominal load.** Enumerated:
`sim/fleet.py`'s UGV profile carries a 5QI-85 DL E-STOP (40 B, PDB 5 ms) and,
since `docs/g2-registration.md`, a 5QI-86 UL twin. `scripts/g2_ul_stop.py`
runs them at `n_ues=8` with no flood in either direction.

### The missing part is SIMULTANEITY, and it is measured

`aperiodic_event` is *"a per-slot Bernoulli-thinned Poisson process"* drawn
**independently per flow**. Counting, over 8 robots and 40,000 slots:

| STOP rate | events | distinct slots | **slots with >1 robot firing together** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.2 Hz** (`sim/fleet.py`'s real cadence) | 17 | 17 | **0** |
| 5 Hz (`g2_ul_stop.py`'s diagnostic rate) | 389 | 386 | 3 |
| 20 Hz | 1 588 | 1 564 | 22 |

**At the real cadence no slot has ever held two STOPs.** GT-1.2's own
functional view — *"Two assets receive STOP **simultaneously**, so this also
exercises same-slot DL contention for the two highest-priority packets in the
cell"* — names same-slot contention as the point of the test, and it has never
occurred in anything this repository has run. The 3 coincidences at 5 Hz are
accidental pairs among 8 independent draws, not a disconnect stopping a fleet.

---

## (c) THE FINDING: the current row's check could not have failed

5QI 85's **standardised PDB is 5 ms**. G2's bound is **100 ms**. These are
different quantities and `sim/buffer.py::expire()` enforces the first: a chunk
older than its PDB is dropped and recorded `complete=False`, and
`sim/messages.py::message_latency_percentiles_ms` counts only `complete` ones.

**So a STOP that misses 5 ms never enters a latency statistic at all.**
Measured — N=8, saturating DL flood, cap 2, 40,000 slots, STOP at 20 Hz:

| condition | arm | delivered | **discarded** | p98 | **MAX delivered** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DL flood | PF | 1 607 | 1 | 3.25 | **5.25 ms** |
| | **Reservation** | 1 520 | **87** | 5.00 | **5.25 ms** |
| | TwoTier | 1 608 | 0 | 3.00 | **4.75 ms** |
| DL + UL flood | PF | 1 544 | 0 | 3.25 | **5.00 ms** |
| | **Reservation** | 1 474 | **70** | 5.25 | **5.25 ms** |
| | TwoTier | 1 544 | 0 | 3.00 | **4.50 ms** |

**The maximum delivered STOP latency is 5.25 ms — the PDB plus one slot — in
every arm and every condition. Nothing scored this way can approach 100 ms.**

**And the failures are in the data as drops.** Reservation lost **87 of
1 607** STOPs while its p98 read 5.00 ms, a clean pass against a 100 ms bound.
**The arm that loses a robot and the arm that loses none score identically.**

This is the project's could-have-failed rule at its sharpest: the check reads
delivered-message latency, the failure mode is non-delivery, and **they do not
intersect**. It is also the same shape as G1's — a guarantee scored on a
statistic that cannot express its own failure — arrived at from the opposite
direction.

### What follows for the scoring, and it is not a metric tweak

**A trial is a MISS if the robot did not receive its STOP within 100 ms.** On
a 5 ms bearer there is no third case: a STOP is either delivered (necessarily
≤ 5.25 ms) or discarded. **So the miss count IS the discard count**, and the
latency distribution is a separate, reported figure rather than the criterion.

**And the campaign runs a DIAGNOSTIC beside the faithful cell.** With the STOP
bearer's PDB lifted to the clause's own 100 ms, a STOP the faithful cell
discards is instead delivered, and its latency answers the question an
operator actually has: *when would that robot have stopped?* The faithful
configuration cannot answer it, because it threw the packet away. Neither
configuration alone is the result; the pair is.

## 1. What was added, and what it makes live

**`sim/traffic.py::scripted_burst`** — a burst at each slot in an explicit,
shared list.
- **Consumers:** `sim/scenarios/g2.py`; through it `scripts/g2_stress.py`,
  the flow-key collision sweep and its coverage assertion, and the GBR
  offered-load invariant.
- **Becomes live that was inert:** nothing existing. It makes
  **simultaneity structural** — a shared list cannot drift apart when someone
  later adds jitter to one flow, which is exactly how `aperiodic_event` looks
  correct and is not.
- **Duplicates:** `sim/scenarios/g11.py::StopDrill` fires ONE burst by gating a
  periodic flow to a one-period window. This is the general form with `n`
  instants. **G11 is deliberately not refactored onto it** — that would move a
  published 7.2 M-slot artefact for no measurement gain.
- **The guard caught it:** `LOAD_PARAMS` refuses a kind it cannot scale, and
  `test_load_params_covers_every_traffic_kind_the_model_dispatches_on` scans
  `_gen`'s AST, so the entry was forced rather than remembered.

**`sim/scenarios/g2.py`** — GT-1.2's cell.
- **Consumers:** `scripts/g2_stress.py`, `sim/tests/test_g2_scenario.py`, and
  both category sweeps.
- **Becomes live:** the **same-slot DL contention** GT-1.2 is written to test,
  for the first time; and **a cell saturated in both directions**, which no
  scenario in the repo had.
- **Duplicates / reuses rather than rewrites:** `sim/scenarios/g1.py`'s
  saturating 5QI-9 DL flood and its fixed-instrument-population discipline;
  `sim/scenarios/g12.py`'s DL-9/UL-8 de-aliasing rule; `sim/workload.py`'s
  `scale_committed_load` and `min_bytes_per_period_for_gfbr`. The committed
  profile is G1's shape, so G1's and G2's cells differ only in what GT-1.2
  adds.
- **It uses the SHARED horizon guard**, `schedule_guard.require_horizon`, and
  needs no `NO_SCHEDULE` exemption — unlike G1, this scenario really does have
  a scripted schedule. `minimum_horizon_slots` derives from the last trial's
  **scoring window**, not its trigger: a disconnect that fires one slot before
  the horizon has fired without being answerable.

## 2. A SECOND loss mechanism, found by a cross-check the campaign carries

**The scorer counts a miss as non-delivery. The campaign also records
`stop_bytes_dropped` independently. On 20 of the first 514 runs they
DISAGREE — `missed = 1` with `dropped = 0 B`.**

**That is not scorer error, and my first explanation for it was WRONG.**

**I hypothesised HARQ retry exhaustion** — CLAUDE.md names it as a distinct
loss mode, a TB that used all `harq_round_max` attempts and still failed. It
was a plausible second mechanism and it is **measured at ZERO**: over 108
runs at N ∈ {12,16}, 451 misses decompose as **434 PDB-expired, 0
HARQ-exhausted, 17 unaccounted**. Recorded because a hypothesis about
behaviour is worth nothing until it is run, and this one was written down
before it was.

**What the 17 actually are, measured:** the divisor was the wrong
instrument. `bytes_dropped_pdb // 40` assumes a STOP is lost whole; **17 of
the 26 disagreeing rows have a dropped-byte count that is NOT a multiple of
40** — 13 B, 15 B, 34 B. Those are STOPs that got a **partial grant**: some
bytes delivered before the 5 ms deadline, the remainder expired. The message
is incomplete, so it is correctly a miss, and the byte counter correctly
records only the fraction that expired.

**So the dispositions are:**

| disposition | how it shows | the robot |
|---|---|---|
| delivered inside the deadline | a completion in the trial's window | stops |
| **expired whole** | `bytes_dropped_pdb` a multiple of 40 | never gets it |
| **expired partial** | `bytes_dropped_pdb` NOT a multiple of 40 | gets a fragment, which is not a STOP |
| **never enqueued** | offered bytes never reach a queue (a source-gated UE) | **MEASURED AT ZERO** — 0 of 120 runs |
| **HARQ retry exhaustion** | `bytes_harq_lost` | **MEASURED AT ZERO** — 0 of 451 misses |

**The miss count itself was right throughout** — non-delivery is
non-delivery, and the scorer reads completions, not counters. What the
cross-check corrected is the **attribution**, twice: once away from HARQ
(which does not happen at all here) and once toward partial delivery (which
the byte counter can only show if you stop dividing by the whole payload).

**Caught because two independent measures were carried and compared**, not
because either was suspected. That is the cheap version of this project's
decompose-before-attributing rule: record a second observable for the same
event and diff it, rather than trusting one counter's name.

**The split is measured separately** (108 runs at N ∈ {12,16}, `n_stop`
∈ {2,4,12}, both caps, 3 seeds) and reported in the result document beside
the miss counts, so no reader can take one mechanism's name as covering all
of them.

**The decomposition is now closed.** Every STOP in this cell is either
delivered inside the deadline, expired whole, or expired after partial
delivery. The two mechanisms I went looking for — HARQ retry exhaustion and
a source-gated UE never enqueuing the packet — are **both measured at
zero**. Stating that is worth as much as the positive finding: it means the
5 ms deadline is the ONLY thing losing STOPs here, so §5.1's lifted-PDB
result covers all of them and not merely most.

**The transferable part is the cross-check, not the mechanism.** Two
independent observables were recorded for the same event and diffed; the
diff was 26 rows of 1 046, and following it corrected a mechanism claim I
had already written into this document. Neither counter was suspected, and
neither alone would have shown anything wrong.

## 3. The staleness guard fired on G1's and G10's claims, and it was right

Adding `scripted_burst` to `sim/traffic.py` and its `LOAD_PARAMS` entry
changed the scoped code hash, so **`verify_claims --check` marked seven
claims STALE** — G1's four and G10's three, whose artefacts were produced
earlier the same day.

**Every failing claim's VALUE still re-derived exactly.** Only the stamp
differed. That is the guard's own stated purpose:

> A green `verify_claims` is evidence the prose matches its source, and is
> NOT evidence the source matches the code.

**Two honest responses, and the cheap one is wrong here.** Marking them
`historical:` asserts the addition is inert; **re-running measures it.** And
this project has a standing reason to prefer the measurement: a new traffic
kind *should* be inert for scenarios that do not use it, but "should" is a
hypothesis, and one hypothesis in this same campaign (HARQ retry exhaustion,
§2) was already written down before being run and turned out to be wrong.

**The corpus was already clean** — 20 records byte-identical — which covers
the driver and scheduler path on three scenarios but not G1's own. So both
campaigns were re-run from scratch, with the previous artefacts preserved
and diffed.

**RESULT: the addition is INERT.** Both campaigns re-ran on the new code
hash (`cd84ef3b7ba88103` → `a83985dcb0307f34`):

| campaign | rows | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| G1's grid | **960** | **identical on all 14 scored fields** (timing excluded by name) |
| G10's boundary | **270** | **identical on every field** |

**And G10's boundary re-derives unchanged: PF 12 / Reservation 6 / TwoTier 7,
no arm non-monotone.** **A stale stamp became a bit-identity check on a new
traffic kind across a whole campaign** — worth more than the `historical:`
note it replaced, and worth more than the re-run alone would have been,
because it is now a *measured* statement rather than a plausible one.

**One trap avoided, and it is worth stating because the runner makes it
easy.** `scripts/g1_stress.py` re-enters banked rows from its ledger, so
simply re-invoking it would have written a **new stamp over old rows** —
an artefact claiming to have been produced by code that never produced it.
`RunLedger`'s config key does not include the code state, so nothing would
have caught it. The ledger was moved aside first.
