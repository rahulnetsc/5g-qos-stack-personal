## Clause 2 — the finding

| arm | B's camera delivered | × MFBR | within tol at 5 % / 10 % / 25 % |
|---|---|---|---|
| PF | 8.42 Mbps | 0.92–1.12× | 5/10, 8/10, **10/10** |
| **Reservation** | **16.20 Mbps** | **1.91–2.09×** | **0/10, 0/10, 0/10** |
| **TwoTier** | **16.22 Mbps** | **2.00–2.09×** | **0/10, 0/10, 0/10** |

**Both QoS-aware arms deliver essentially the full 2× MFBR the aggressor
offers. Neither contains it at any tolerance, including 25 %.**

### The inversion is the headline

**PF has no MFBR concept at all and contains the aggressor better than the
two arms that implement the clamp.** PF holds B's camera near 1× MFBR
because proportional fairness bounds any one UE's share; Reservation and
TwoTier hold it at 2× *because their clamp does not bound delivery*.

**An operator reading "entitlement is enforced" from the presence of MFBR
configuration would be reading the opposite of what happens.**

## The mechanism, read from the C rather than inferred from the port

The clamp exists in the deployed C at three sites — `ia_p5g_scheduler.c:
2663-2665`, `gNB_scheduler_ulsch.c:2248-2250`, `gNB_scheduler_dlsch.c:
397-399` — and the port reproduces all three faithfully:

```c
int _max_burst = (int)(_c->gbr_ul_max / 8) / (_spf_ul * 100) * 2;
if (_max_burst < _obl * 2) _max_burst = _obl * 2;
if (_target > _max_burst) _target = _max_burst;
sched_ctrl->ul_total_target_bytes += _target;
} else {
    sched_ctrl->ul_total_target_bytes +=
        sched_ctrl->estimated_ul_buffer_per_lcg[_lcg];
}
```

**What is clamped is `_target` — the GBR obligation.** The demand above it is
not dropped; in the port's transcription of the same block,
`overflow = lcg_estimate - target; be_bytes += overflow`. **The excess is
re-classified as best-effort and remains eligible for delivery.**

So `gbr_ul_max` bounds **how much of a flow is treated as guaranteed**. It
does not bound **how much the flow receives**. The DL site's own comment says
as much — *"cap at 2x per-slot MBR to prevent cell monopolisation"* — a
burst-shaping intent, not a rate ceiling.

## The registered second read, and its limit stated

Registered: *"if clause 2 fails, compare B's delivered excess against cell
utilisation. Excess that vanishes as utilisation → 1.0 confirms BE-path
delivery; excess that persists at saturation refutes it."*

| load_mult | UL utilisation | Reservation | TwoTier | PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 0.933 | 2.02× | 2.03× | 1.05× |
| 1.5 | 0.932 | 2.03× | 2.01× | 1.07× |
| 2.5 | 0.932 | **2.14×** | **2.14×** | 1.07× |

**The excess does not vanish — it grows.** But the read is **only partly
conclusive, and the reason is stated rather than glossed: UL utilisation
could not be driven above ~0.933 with this knob**, so *"at saturation"* was
never actually reached; extra offered load is queued or dropped rather than
raising utilisation.

**What that does establish:** across a 2.5× range of offered load at ~93 %
utilisation, the aggressor's excess persists and grows. So it is **not**
mere spare-capacity opportunism — **the BE overflow is winning capacity in
competition with other traffic.** My registered mechanism said delivery above
MFBR should appear *"exactly when spare capacity exists"*; that was too weak,
and the correction is recorded rather than quietly dropped.

## Clauses 1 and 3 — both pass, and clause 3 is the one that could have been missed

Asset A's telemetry p98 never breaches its 100 ms PDB on any arm or seed
(max 47.25 ms), and A's camera M05 fails 0/10 everywhere. **So the excess is
genuinely not exported to A** — the isolation half of GT-4.3 holds.

**Clause 3 is the clause easiest to drop and it also passes**: B's *own*
telemetry (5QI-1, PDB 100 ms) never breaches — max 65.75 ms on TwoTier.
Containment holds inside the misbehaving asset because both arms' UL rankings
put the lower `pdb_ms` ahead of the camera, exactly as registered.

## FOR THE HARDWARE TEAM

> **`gbr_ul_max` / `gbr_dl_max` (MFBR) bounds entitlement, not throughput.**
> The clamp at `ia_p5g_scheduler.c:2663-2665` (and the matching UL/DL sites
> in `gNB_scheduler_ulsch.c:2248` / `gNB_scheduler_dlsch.c:397`) limits
> `_target`, the GBR obligation. Demand above it falls through to the
> best-effort accumulator and stays deliverable. **A UE whose encoder is
> mis-set to twice its MFBR receives twice its MFBR**, measured at 2.02–2.14×
> across 10 seeds and three load levels on both the two-tier and reservation
> schedulers.
>
> **The other assets are unharmed** — that half of the guarantee holds — and
> so is the misbehaving asset's own telemetry. **What does not hold is
> "entitlement is a ceiling".**
>
> This is read from the C's own source and reproduced by a faithful port; it
> is the same class of finding as the Tier-1.5 floor being gated on the
> condition whose absence defines its own fault. **If MFBR is meant to be a
> delivery ceiling, no code path currently implements it.**

## Consequence for the guarantee set

**G7 is now MEASURED — nine guarantees carry a verdict rather than eight.**
Its verdict is **FAIL on clause 2** for both QoS-aware arms, PASS on clauses
1 and 3, and the failure is a **product** property rather than a simulator
gap.
