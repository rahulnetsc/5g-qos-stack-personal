## 0. FLAGGED SEPARATELY — changes a verdict, not a number

### 0.1 ✓verified — the PDCCH bound that anchors the regime story is not what the simulator does

**`average_agg_level = 4` does not exist in `sim/` or `scheduler/`.** Every
arm computes CCE cost through `scheduler/link.py::cce_aggregation_level(snr_db)`,
a staircase returning **1 at snr ≥ 20 dB**. The base point is
`_BASE_SNR_DB = 20.0` with `stationary_std_db = 1.5`, so:

| | P(AL=1) | P(AL=2) | P(AL=4) | effective AL | U-slot capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| computed | 0.5000 | 0.5000 | 3.2×10⁻⁵ | **1.500** | **32 / 1.5 ≈ 21 grants** |
| documented | — | — | — | 4 | 32 / 4 = **8** |

**The measured N=8 separation boundary is real. Its stated explanation is
not supported by the code.** What this reaches:

- **D4-3 is scored a HIT on the numeric coincidence** that 32/4 = 8, and
  D4-4's "the boundary lies in (4,8] and lands on §1.1's predicted PDCCH
  bound" inherits it.
- **§0.3 defers the whole `min_rb` question** on the grounds that the PDCCH
  bound binds first. At AL ≈ 1.5 the follower-budget bound (55/5 = 11,
  `scheduler/reservation.py:505`) would bind first — **`min_rb` is
  plausibly the binding axis at the deployed value**, and it is the axis the
  stage-1 cap dropped and the map records as untested by choice.
- **The regime map hands the hardware campaign** "expect the knee between
  N=4 and N=8" and "testing N=2 again would reproduce a null this map now
  explains" on this basis.
- **And it reaches my own G11 work**: §7.4 and both G11 rows argue N=2 is
  "4× below the binding PDCCH bound (32 CCE / AL 4 = 8)". **That arithmetic
  is wrong.** The N=2 *specification* finding survives on its measured half
  — C1 passes 10/10 on every arm at N=2 and D4-4 measured zero qualifying
  separations at N=4 — but its mechanistic half must be withdrawn.

CLAUDE.md's own known-issues list already flagged the ambiguity ("decide
deliberately whether the sim models it fixed or SNR-dependent"). **The sim
chose SNR-dependent and the plan documented fixed.**

### 0.1a SCOPED — every place the bound is used, and what survives

**The measured boundary is not in question.** What is in question is the
*explanation* attached to it and the *deferral* built on that explanation.

**Re-derive both candidate bounds on the corrected arithmetic:**

| bound | formula | documented | corrected |
|---|---|---|---|
| follower budget | `prb_count / min_rb` = 55/5 | 11 | **11** (unchanged) |
| PDCCH | `U-slot CCE / AL` = 32/AL | 32/4 = **8** | 32/1.5 ≈ **21** |
| **measured boundary** | — | **8** | **8** |

**The consequence is sharper than "the number was wrong": on the corrected
arithmetic BOTH candidate bounds sit ABOVE the measured boundary (11 and
21 against 8), so NEITHER explains it.** H1's own formula
`N_crit = min(55/min_rb, CCE/AL)` now predicts **11**, and the boundary is
at 8. The measured result did not confirm the prediction — it undershoots
the nearer of the two bounds by 3 UEs, and that gap is currently
unexplained.

**A further reason the explanation cannot hold as stated: there is no
single PDCCH bound.** `cce_aggregation_level` is a function of instantaneous
SNR, so AL varies per UE per slot. At the base point it is 1 or 2 with equal
probability. Under `snr_spread_db` or blockage it would differ again. "The
PDCCH bound" is a constant the simulator does not have.

#### The serious consequence: `min_rb` may be the axis that matters

§0.3 defers `min_rb` on the grounds that it *cannot* move the boundary
below ≈7 because the PDCCH bound binds first. **If the follower budget
binds first, `min_rb` moves the boundary across the whole swept range**, and
two facts already on record stop being anomalies and become the expected
result:

- **stage 1 swept `min_rb` ∈ {1, 5, 20} and it separated the arms more
  strongly than almost any other axis — score 152.579** — and was dropped
  by the "at most one excursion axis" cap.
- §0.4 already records that **the cap did the narrowing, not the score**,
  and lists `min_rb` among the axes dropped for budget rather than for
  irrelevance.

**So the axis the cap dropped may be the axis that determines the number
this project exists to locate**, and the reason it was safe to drop it was
an explanation the code does not support. That is a scoping consequence,
not a numeric one, and it is the part of this finding worth acting on
first.

**What it does NOT license.** It does not show `min_rb` *does* bind — 11 is
still above the measured 8, so the follower budget does not explain the
boundary either. The honest position is: **the boundary is measured, both
candidate explanations are now excluded, and locating the real one needs
`min_rb` as an axis** — which is the experiment §0.3 deferred.
